They see high-quality outputs—detailed reports, accurate predictions, strategic recommendations—and assume these reflect the company's cognitive capacity. They don't see the external cognitive infrastructure that makes these outputs possible, nor the dependency the company has developed on it. Opacity stems from AI's black-box nature: managers see inputs and outputs, but not the internal process. The company believes it's making autonomous decisions when it's actually following recommendations from a system it doesn't understand. It's like a pilot blindly trusting the autopilot without knowing how it works or when it might fail. This opacity is the most dangerous aspect: the company doesn't know it has lost cognitive autonomy because it never had to exercise it. It has shifted directly from dependence on human knowledge—visible and manageable—to dependence on algorithmic inference—invisible and uncontrollable. The company thinks it uses AI as a tool, when in fact AI uses the company as a channel to extract value from its cognitive infrastructure. El capitalismo industrial extraía valor del tiempo de trabajo; el algorítmico extrae valor del tiempo de vida.
