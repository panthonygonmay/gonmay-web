Este encuadre no es neutral; refleja los sesgos y prioridades del proveedor del modelo, que pueden no alinearse con los intereses de la empresa cliente. To understand this mechanism, we can turn to Lakoff's (2004) concept of framing in political language. Lakoff argued that the words and metaphors we use are not neutral vehicles of meaning; they frame our understanding of reality, highlighting certain aspects and hiding others. Framing is not just rhetoric; it is cognition: it determines what information we process and how we interpret it. When a politician speaks of 'tax relief' rather than 'tax cuts for the rich,' they are not choosing different words; they activate different cognitive frames that lead to different conclusions. Foundation models operate similarly, but with a crucial difference: their framing is neither explicit nor negotiable. When a company uses a model to analyze a problem, the model does not just give an answer; it frames the problem in a particular way. It selects which variables are relevant, which causal relationships matter, which metrics should be used to evaluate success. This framing is not neutral; it reflects the biases and priorities of the model provider, which may not align with the interests of the client company.
