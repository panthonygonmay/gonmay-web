They don't see the external cognitive infrastructure that makes these outputs possible, nor the dependency the company has developed on it. Opacity stems from AI's black-box nature: managers see inputs and outputs, but not the internal process. The company believes it's making autonomous decisions when it's actually following recommendations from a system it doesn't understand. It's like a pilot blindly trusting the autopilot without knowing how it works or when it might fail. This opacity is the most dangerous aspect: the company doesn't know it has lost cognitive autonomy because it never had to exercise it. It has shifted directly from dependence on human knowledge—visible and manageable—to dependence on algorithmic inference—invisible and uncontrollable.
