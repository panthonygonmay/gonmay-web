Decidir si una cláusula contractual es razonable dadas las circunstancias del cliente, evaluar si una tendencia de mercado es estructural o coyuntural, o determinar qué supuestos utilizar en un modelo financiero son ejemplos de juicio contextual. Los sistemas de IA actuales, basados en aprendizaje profundo, pueden aproximar el juicio contextual en dominios acotados, pero fallan sistemáticamente cuando el contexto cambia de manera no prevista en los datos de entrenamiento. Deciding whether a contract clause is reasonable given a client's circumstances, assessing whether a market trend is structural or cyclical, or determining which assumptions to use in a financial model are examples of contextual judgment. This layer is more resistant to automation because it requires context understanding, accumulated experience, and the ability to weigh intangible factors. Current AI systems based on deep learning can approximate contextual judgment in bounded domains, but they systematically fail when context shifts in ways not anticipated in training data. The third layer is expert intuition: deeply tacit cognitive processes that allow pattern recognition, anomaly detection, and hypothesis formation without explicit reasoning. An experienced surgeon who 'feels' something is wrong during an operation, an investment manager who 'intuits' a market is overvalued, or a litigator who 'senses' a witness is hiding information are operating at this layer. Expert intuition is the result of thousands of hours of practice, exposure to varied cases, and continuous feedback; it is the most valuable and hardest to automate form of cognitive work.
