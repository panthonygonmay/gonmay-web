AI, even if it simulates resource limitations, possesses no ontological anguish. It does not fear disappearing nor feels life's urgency because it does not experience the pressure of nothingness. The 'new axiom' that enables Vita Contemplativa is not a logical conclusion but a phenomenological truth. A human axiom is a statement about existence born from being alive, feeling pain, loving, and knowing one will die. AI can combine concepts to create a sentence that seems profound, but it cannot generate a truth born from vulnerability. It can write a perfect poem about grief by analyzing ten thousand elegies, but the poem of someone who has lost a child carries a truth weight that lies not in meter but in the resonance of real loss. AI optimizes the form of grief; humans contemplate the essence of pain. Generating axioms based on experience turns contemplation into an ontological duty, not a luxury. In a society where AI solves any technical problem, only the problem of meaning remains.6:T418,Workers performing strategic tasks —problem definition, interpreting results, decision-making— require contextual human judgment and are hard to automate. At the other end, workers performing operational AI supervision —validating outputs, correcting errors, tweaking parameters— require technical skills but no deep domain expertise. This polarization is not temporary; it stems from the fundamental asymmetry between AI's ability to execute and its inability to define problems. Strategic workers are few and highly valued; operational workers are many and easily replaceable. The professional middle class, which lived precisely in the space between them, disappears. The typical knowledge worker —neither pure strategist nor pure operator— is left without a clear role in the new cognitive division of labor. Their disappearance is not a marginal adjustment; it is a transformation of the occupational structure that has sustained social mobility and political stability in developed economies.
