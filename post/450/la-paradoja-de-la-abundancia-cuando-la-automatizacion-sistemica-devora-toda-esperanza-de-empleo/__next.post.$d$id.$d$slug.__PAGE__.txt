It is not that one sector—agriculture—employs fewer people while other sectors—industry, services—absorb displaced workers. It is that the capacity to produce without human labor extends to all sectors of the economy, from manufacturing to cognitive services. There is no refuge sector that can absorb displaced workers because all sectors are being automated simultaneously. This phenomenon, which economists call generalized automation, is historically unprecedented. The post-labor transition can occur in a fraction of the time it took for the agricultural-industrial shift, with no alternative sectors to absorb the labor surplus. There is no "new economy" that can absorb the displaced from the "old economy" because the new economy is also intensive in algorithmic capital and minimally intensive in human labor. The post-labor firm faces an aggregate demand problem it cannot solve on its own.6:T54d,¿Se descubre la automatización? No hay una respuesta correcta; cada opción tiene costes y beneficios que dependen del mercado, el producto y la base de clientes. Algunas empresas algorítmicas resuelven esta tensión siendo transparentes sobre su naturaleza automatizada. Comunican explícitamente que utilizan IA para la atención al cliente, pero garantizan supervisión humana para casos complejos. Esta estrategia construye confianza mediante honestidad, pero puede reducir la disposición de los clientes a interactuar si perciben que no hay un humano detrás. Otras optan por mantener la ilusión de humanidad, utilizando agentes que imitan el lenguaje y los patrones de comunicación humana. Esta estrategia maximiza la efectividad de la interacción a corto plazo, pero corre el riesgo de una crisis de confianza mayor si se descubre la automatización. Ambas estrategias tienen riesgos y no existe una solución universalmente óptima. La elección depende de factores como el tipo de producto, el perfil del cliente, la cultura del mercado y la tolerancia al riesgo del fundador. Lo que está claro es que la gestión de la identidad en la empresa algorítmica no es un problema técnico, sino un problema estratégico y, en última instancia, filosófico: ¿qué significa ser auténtico cuando no hay seres humanos detrás de la interacción?7:T46e,Should automation be revealed? There is no correct answer; each option has costs and benefits that depend on market, product, and customer base. Some algorithmic firms resolve this tension by being transparent about their automated nature. They explicitly state they use AI for customer service, but guarantee human oversight for complex cases. This strategy builds trust through honesty, but may reduce customers' willingness to interact if they perceive no human behind it. Others choose to maintain the illusion of humanity, using agents that mimic human language and communication patterns. This strategy maximizes short-term interaction effectiveness, but risks a greater trust crisis if automation is discovered. Both strategies have risks and there is no universally optimal solution. The choice depends on factors such as product type, customer profile, market culture, and founder's risk tolerance. What is clear is that identity management in the algorithmic firm is not a technical problem, but a strategic and ultimately philosophical one: what does it mean to be authentic when there are no
