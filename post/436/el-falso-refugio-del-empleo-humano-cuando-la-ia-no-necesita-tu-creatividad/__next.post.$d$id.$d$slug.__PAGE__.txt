But if AI can perform complex, creative, and 'human' cognitive tasks, then displacement has no destination. Decoupling becomes structural, not sectoral. The faith that the market will generate new jobs to replace destroyed ones rests on a general equilibrium model that assumes human preferences are limitless and that there will always be tasks humans do better than machines. But if AI matches or exceeds human performance in a growing range of cognitive tasks, the premise of an ever-present 'refuge' for human employment weakens. AI doesn't need to beat humans in all tasks; it only needs to beat them in enough tasks that the opportunity cost of employing humans becomes prohibitive for most firms. In that scenario, decoupling is not marginal or transitory, but the new steady state of the economy. The industrial economy could afford to be inefficient with humans because it needed their cognitive capacity.
